Status legend

  1. AnnouncedA company or public body has publicly announced plans for a service or trial in this place. No public-road testing confirmed yet.
  2. Testing with safety driverAutomated vehicles are being tested on public roads with a trained safety driver on board. No public passengers.
  3. Passenger rides with safety driverMembers of the public (paying, free or pre-registered) can ride, with a safety driver or operator on board.

UK rules

UK rules timeline

How the Automated Vehicles Act 2024 is coming into force across Great Britain.

  1. 20 May 2024

    Automated Vehicles Act receives Royal Assent

    The Automated Vehicles Act 2024 became law, setting out a framework for self-driving vehicles, including a permitting scheme for automated passenger services (APS).

    Source: GOV.UK, 20 May 2024 · Last verified 5 Oct 2026

  2. 15 May 2026

    APS permit regulations in force (SI 2026/439)

    The Automated Vehicles (Permits for Automated Passenger Services) Regulations 2026 came into force. They extend to England and Wales and Scotland.

    Source: legislation.gov.uk: SI 2026/439 · Last verified 5 Oct 2026

  3. 22 May 2026

    DVSA opens the APS permit application portal

    Operators can apply to the Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency (DVSA) for a permit to run a taxi-, private hire- or bus-like passenger service using self-driving vehicles without a safety driver.

    Source: DVSA APS portal and GOV.UK, 22 May 2026 · Last verified 5 Oct 2026

  4. 7 January 2027

    Marketing restrictions in force (SI 2026/733)

    The Automated Vehicles (Marketing Restrictions) Regulations 2026 list restricted terms, including “self-driving”, “driverless”, “autonomous” and “drive itself”. Under section 78 of the Act, these may be used to promote or supply road vehicles only in connection with authorised automated vehicles.

    Source: legislation.gov.uk: SI 2026/733 · Last verified 5 Oct 2026

  5. Expected late 2027

    Rest of the Act expected

    The government says the remaining provisions of the Automated Vehicles Act are due to come into force in late 2027.

    Source: GOV.UK: pilot scheme information for applicants · Last verified 5 Oct 2026

Europe

European regulation at a glance

The main rules behind the European entries above.

Germany: Level 4 law and KBA testing approvals

Germany’s Autonomous Driving Act came into force on 28 July 2021. It allows autonomous (Level 4) vehicles to run in regular service on public roads within approved operating areas.

The Federal Motor Transport Authority (KBA) publishes a list of the testing approvals it has granted. In July 2026 Momenta said it had received Germany-wide Level 4 testing approval from the KBA.

The KBA list we checked on 5 October 2026 (dated 18 August 2026) includes a Level 4 testing approval for Momenta Europe GmbH, granted on 8 July 2026 and valid until 31 July 2028, with no local restriction.

Sources: Federal Ministry of Transport (German), KBA list of testing approvals (German), Momenta, 29 Jul 2026 · Last verified 5 Oct 2026

Switzerland: Ordinance on Automated Driving

Since 1 March 2025, three uses of automated driving have been allowed: a motorway pilot, driverless vehicles on approved routes supervised by an operator, and automated parking in designated car parks.

PostBus’s AmiGo vehicles in eastern Switzerland are testing with safety drivers under a special permit from the Federal Roads Office (FEDRO). In the Furttal near Zurich, the iamo pilot, which FEDRO approved for automated driving in November 2025, has carried paying passengers since 28 September 2026 with a safety person in the driver’s seat.

Sources: FEDRO (German), PostBus, 12 Jun 2026, iamo, 24 Sep 2026 (German) · Last verified 5 Oct 2026

EU: type approval for fully automated vehicles (Regulation 2022/1426)

Implementing Regulation (EU) 2022/1426 sets out how the automated driving systems of fully automated vehicles are approved, for uses such as hub-to-hub routes, defined areas and automated valet parking. For now it covers vehicles built in small series.

A companion amendment to the EU type-approval framework caps small-series approvals of fully automated buses and heavy goods vehicles at 1,500 vehicles a year per type.

Sources: EUR-Lex: Regulation (EU) 2022/1426, DfT explanatory memorandum, Jul 2022 (PDF) · Last verified 5 Oct 2026

Netherlands: Tesla FSD (Supervised) approval

Driver assistance (supervised, SAE Level 2), not self-driving

On 10 April 2026 the RDW, the Dutch vehicle authority, granted type approval for Tesla’s FSD (Supervised) in the Netherlands. The RDW says: “A vehicle with FSD Supervised is not self-driving.” The driver remains responsible and must stay in control.

Use in other EU countries needs a majority vote of member states in the responsible EU committee. electrive reported on 28 September 2026 that the vote is not expected before December.

Sources: RDW, 10 Apr 2026, heise online (SAE Level 2), electrive, 28 Sep 2026 · Last verified 5 Oct 2026

Closer to home

What’s next for York, Leeds and Manchester

We found no confirmed self-driving taxi or shuttle trials in York, Leeds or Manchester as of 5 October 2026.

Under the UK’s automated passenger services (APS) scheme, a taxi- or private hire-like service needs the consent of each licensing authority (usually the local council) in the area it would serve, and the authority has six weeks to respond. For a bus-like service in an area with franchised buses, the franchising authority must consent. The Department for Transport also says: “We do not expect deployments in the pilot period to be cross-boundary.”

In January 2025 Auto Express reported that Manchester council officials had agreed in principle for the city to be a future rollout site for Verne. We have found no date, permit or trial announced since, so it is not listed in the table.

Sources: GOV.UK: local authority and transport body roles, Auto Express, 10 Jan 2025 · Last verified 5 Oct 2026

Methodology

How we compile the tracker

  • Sources. We use official sources first: operator and partner press releases, company blogs, regulators and government publications. We use established news outlets only to fill gaps, and we say so when we do. Each entry links to its sources and shows the date we last checked them.
  • Statuses. Each entry shows the most advanced stage we could confirm: Announced, Testing with safety driver, Passenger rides with safety driver, Driverless testing or Driverless commercial. Paused and Ended are shown separately. Where a source’s wording and other reports differ, we use the more cautious status and explain why in the notes.
  • Fleet sizes. We show a fleet size only when the operator, the project or the licensing authority officially states one. Estimates from other sources are left out.
  • What’s included. Self-driving taxi, ride-pooling and shuttle services carrying, or planned to carry, passengers on public roads in the UK and Europe. Driver assistance systems such as Tesla FSD (Supervised) are not self-driving, so they appear only in the regulation box.
  • Updates. We review the sources weekly and record changes in What’s changed. If something is wrong or out of date, please tell us.
  • Independence. We don’t copy data from other trackers, and no company listed has paid for or reviewed its entry.
